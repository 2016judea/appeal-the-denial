Privacy

Last updated November 19, 2026.

Your letter is not stored

On this website the free answer is worked out in your browser. Your letter is not sent to us.

When you open a paid packet, your letter is sent once to build it. It is used for that one request and not saved, logged or shared. Until then it sits only in your browser tab's session storage, which clears when you close the tab.

Inside ChatGPT or Claude, the app gets the letter text the assistant sends it, builds the answer and keeps nothing. ChatGPT and Claude have their own privacy policies for your conversation.

The answer in the chat ends with a link that opens your deadline, your letter and a calendar file on this website. Your letter's details travel in that link after the "#". Browsers never send that part to any server, ours included. The page reads it on your device and removes it from the address bar before any analytics loads.

Deadline tracking

Reminders are a calendar file you add to your own calendar. We don't keep a list of your deadlines.

What we do collect

Visit counts. Vercel Web Analytics counts page views without cookies. PostHog counts visits and a few named button presses (reading a letter, copying it, adding the deadline to a calendar, checkout), with the kind of plan and the kind of denial reason (for example "employer plan", "medical necessity"). Never the letter, a name, an ID number, a date, the insurer or the care that was denied. Click recording and session recording are off on every page.

In ChatGPT or Claude. Each use of the app records one anonymous event: which tool, whether it worked, how long it took, which app called it, and the same plan type and reason kind. Nothing from the letter.

Payments. Stripe handles checkout and keeps your name, email and card details under its own policy. We see that a payment was made, its amount and your email.

Health information

Because we don't store your letter, we hold no health information about you. If that ever changes, for example an opt-in reminder service, this page will say so first, and we'll follow the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, which applies to consumer health apps.
